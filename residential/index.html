No Surprises

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Effective HVAC service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Troy HVAC offers honest, upfront pricing across Troy, covering everything from a worn capacitor to a full system replacement. Every job begins with a careful inspection of your equipment, including its age, tonnage, fuel type, refrigerant charge, and duct condition, followed by a plain-English recommendation with the price spelled out before any work starts.

Many HVAC problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Refrigerant seeps out through pinhole leaks in the evaporator coil, blower bearings wear slowly, and a weakening capacitor gives no warning until the compressor won't start on the first hot day. If you notice weak airflow, rising energy bills, ice on the refrigerant lines, or rooms that never reach the thermostat setting, call for an inspection right away. An early diagnosis often keeps a repairable issue from becoming a replacement.

Do you know how old your system is? When a furnace or AC gets past the 12-year mark, it's worth planning the next one before the current one decides for you. Emergency replacements happen on the worst days, in heat waves and cold snaps, when options are few and everyone is uncomfortable. Our team knows how the Troy climate wears on equipment and will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Preventive Care

Maintenance Plans for Troy Homes

A Troy HVAC maintenance visit is far more than a quick filter swap. Our technician cleans the coils, verifies the refrigerant charge, tests capacitors and contactors, inspects the burners and heat exchanger, clears the condensate drain, puts in a fresh filter, and checks static pressure in the duct system. Small problems are caught while they're inexpensive to correct, and the equipment keeps running clean and efficient.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with spring cooling tune-ups and fall heating tune-ups for most systems. This is especially important in dusty areas, where dirty condenser coils choke heat rejection and drive up energy use. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your equipment's age, your household's comfort demands, and your local climate.

Consider maintenance as protection for equipment you've already bought. Each tune-up keeps the coils clean, the charge correct, and the filter fresh, which lowers monthly energy bills while easing the strain on the compressor and blower. For most families, the plan costs far less than an early replacement.

Proper Sizing

The Right Size System for Your Home

Proper sizing is one of the most overlooked parts of any install. Before quoting new equipment, Troy HVAC works through the details that drive your home's heating and cooling load: square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct layout. The result is a system that runs in long, efficient cycles, controls humidity, and keeps every room at the temperature you set.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising comfort demands. A single-stage system that served a small household rarely satisfies a finished basement and two added bedrooms, and a modern two-stage or variable-speed heat pump can hold tighter temperatures while cutting energy use significantly. If you're weighing furnace-and-AC against a heat pump, or ducted against ductless, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: SEER2 efficiency ratings, operating cost, electrical and venting requirements, and upfront installation work, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

Heating and Cooling Problems We Solve in Troy

Warm air from the vents, a furnace that won't ignite, a frozen evaporator coil, leaking refrigerant: every one of these problems has a different cause and needs its own repair. Troy HVAC tracks down the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that short-cycles, a thermostat that loses its settings, a clogged condensate drain, or a mini-split flashing error codes.

The right repair depends entirely on the part that failed. On cooling systems, that usually means a capacitor, contactor, or fan motor. On furnaces, it tends to be an igniter, flame sensor, or control board. Leaky ductwork pushing conditioned air into the attic is sealed and repaired, and we recover and recharge the refrigerant once the leak itself is repaired. A few of these problems are simply a nuisance, but a cracked heat exchanger or a clogged condensate drain can be a real safety or water-damage risk, so we treat it with urgency.
