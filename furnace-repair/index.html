Every time your thermostat calls for heat, the furnace runs a fixed checklist. The inducer motor starts and proves draft by closing a pressure switch, the hot surface igniter heats up, the gas valve opens, the burners light, and the flame sensor confirms there is fire. Only after all of that does the blower start pushing warm air into the house. Finding the step that fails is most of the job. Around Troy, MI, the usual failure points are a flame sensor fouled with oxide, an igniter cracked by years of heating and cooling, a pressure switch that stays open because of a weak inducer or a clogged condensate trap on a high-efficiency unit, and control boards with burned relays. Each one halts the startup, and from the hallway they all look alike.

Troy HVAC repairs furnaces by testing the sequence, not guessing at it. Our experienced technicians verify each stage with a meter rather than a hunch: igniter resistance, flame signal in microamps, pressure switch operation against actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor health. Every visit includes the safety fundamentals on a fuel-burning appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak-testing of gas connections, and flue draft verification, because the difference between a furnace problem and a furnace hazard is not visible from the thermostat. We measure temperature rise against the data plate before we leave so the fire and the airflow are matched the way the manufacturer intended.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we work on them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are stocked on every truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. When to Call for Furnace Repair in Troy

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Keep an eye out for the following clues that it's time to bring in an expert:

  • Flames that start, last a few seconds, and go out again and again
  • Air moving through the vents that never gets warm
  • Water, rust stains, or black soot around the flue or cabinet
  • A pilot or burner flame that is lazy yellow instead of crisp blue
  • Short cycling every few minutes without reaching the setpoint
  • Grinding or high-pitched squeals from the blower
  • A bang or boom when the burners ignite late
  • The igniter glows and clicks, yet no flame ever appears

Why Professional Furnace Repair Matters

A furnace combines gas, flame, high voltage, and a flue in one cabinet, and the failure that matters most is the one you cannot see from outside: a compromised heat exchanger passing combustion gases into the air your blower pushes through the house. Delayed ignition that booms at light-off stresses that exchanger further every morning it goes unfixed. Meanwhile the diagnostic traps stack up: a furnace that trips its high limit may have a bad switch, or it may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a dying blower, and replacing the switch in the second case just removes a safety while leaving the fault.

Our experienced technicians treat every symptom as a question about the whole system. Flame signal gets measured rather than assumed, so a healthy sensor is never replaced while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve skates by. Draft is verified at the pressure switch and the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, which is how airflow problems get caught before they shorten the exchanger's life. And the gas side is leak-tested with instruments, not a sniff and a shrug. The result is a furnace that has been proven safe and correct under a full cycle, and an honest report if the unit is nearing the point where replacement beats another repair.

How to Prevent Furnace Repair Problems

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Make sure the furnace has clear combustion air; a packed utility room starves the burners
  • Schedule service each fall to catch worn igniters and dirty sensors before cold weather arrives
  • Replace the filter every month while the furnace is working hard, since it costs less than any other part
  • Keep storage, lint, and especially chlorine-based products away from the furnace closet
  • Notice how the furnace sounds at startup, because a quiet light-off is healthy and a boom is not
  • Test your CO detectors monthly and make sure each floor has one
  • Keep the condensate trap and drain clear on high-efficiency units to prevent lockouts
