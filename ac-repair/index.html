A humming outdoor unit with a fan that will not turn is the classic summer service call in Troy, MI, and the cause is usually a failed run capacitor. That inexpensive component starts the compressor and fan, fades for years without complaint, and then quits under peak load. It is far from the only suspect, though. Contactors pit and stop passing clean power, condenser fan bearings dry out, small refrigerant leaks drain away cooling capacity, and evaporator coils ice over when a clogged filter strangles airflow.

Troy HVAC repairs air conditioners diagnosis-first. Our experienced technicians measure before they replace: capacitors metered for microfarads against the label rating, compressor and fan amp draws checked against the data plate, refrigerant charge evaluated by superheat and subcooling rather than a quick glance at the gauges, and the evaporator coil, blower wheel, and filter inspected whenever icing is in the picture. That matters because the same warm-air complaint can be four different faults at four very different costs, and topping off refrigerant without finding the leak just rents you cold air until it escapes again.

Central air, heat pumps running in cooling mode, and ductless mini splits from every major brand are all in our wheelhouse. When to Call for AC Repair in Troy

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. These are the signals our technicians see most often when a problem is underway:

  • Water leaking around the indoor unit or from the ceiling below it
  • Rising electric costs paired with a sticky, uncomfortable house
  • Rapid on-off cycling that never brings the temperature down
  • Room-temperature air from the registers even though the AC is running
  • An iced-over evaporator coil or frozen refrigerant lines
  • A burning smell or repeated breaker trips when the AC starts
  • Clicking or humming outside with no fan movement
  • Bubbling or hissing noises that point to a possible refrigerant leak

The Benefits of Professional AC Repair

An air conditioner puts several hazards into one cabinet. The capacitor can deliver a painful shock long after the breaker is off, the condensing unit is wired for 240 volts, and the refrigerant circuit is a sealed, pressurized loop that federal rules forbid venting. Servicing it properly takes gauges, a recovery machine, and a vacuum pump, not a DIY recharge can. Diagnosis has its own traps. A frozen coil is as likely to be an airflow issue as a charge issue, and recharging a system that still leaks throws money away while moisture and acid work on the compressor.

What you get from a pro is a repair that holds. Our experienced technicians find leaks with dye and electronic detectors, base every charge decision on superheat and subcooling, test electrical parts with a meter before condemning them, and measure static pressure and inspect the blower and filter whenever airflow is in question. Parts-cannon fixes tend to bring you back in a few weeks. Ours are built to last through the heat. How to Prevent AC Repair Problems

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Never enclose the condenser; it needs open space on every side to shed heat
  • Listen for new buzzing or clicking at startup, the classic sound of a dying capacitor
  • Flush the condensate drain line with the season's first use to head off clogs
  • Have the AC serviced every spring, ahead of the first real heat
  • Swap filters monthly while the AC is running hard to keep the evaporator coil from icing
  • Rinse the outdoor condenser coil each spring and keep vegetation two feet back
  • Keep supply and return vents open; closing rooms raises static pressure on the blower
