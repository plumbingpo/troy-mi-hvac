Think of a heat pump as an air conditioner that can work in both directions. In summer it pulls heat out of your home; in winter a reversing valve flips the refrigerant flow, and the same compressor draws heat from the outdoor air and brings it inside. Because it moves heat instead of creating it, a heat pump can deliver two to three times more heat energy than the electricity it uses, which is a big reason homeowners in Troy, MI are making the switch as older systems wear out. Today's cold-climate models, built around variable-speed inverter compressors, keep producing useful heat well below freezing, far beyond the heat pumps of twenty years ago.

Great heat pump performance starts with design work, and that is where Troy HVAC earns your trust. A heat pump has to be sized for two loads, summer cooling and winter heating, and the design has to find the balance point where the outdoor temperature meets the unit's capacity curve. Our experienced technicians run the load calculation, compare single-stage, two-stage, and variable-speed inverter equipment against your climate and ductwork, and plan the backup heat properly, either electric strips staged below the balance point or a dual-fuel setup with a gas furnace that takes over in deep cold. We also check your electrical service and ducts, since heat pumps deliver lower supply temperatures than furnaces and need solid airflow to feel comfortable.

The installation itself is where careful habits pay off. We pressure-test and evacuate the line set to a verified vacuum, weigh in the charge and confirm it with superheat and subcooling, and raise the outdoor unit on a stand when your winters call for defrost drainage. The condensate gets proper handling, and the thermostat is set so staging, backup heat lockouts, and defrost behave exactly as the engineering intends. Commissioning covers both modes, with every reading recorded. If you want a heat pump installed by people who measure, call (248) 278-1826 for an assessment and a firm quote.

Why Hire a Professional for Heat Pump Installation?

No residential system is less forgiving of shortcuts than a heat pump, since every error shows up in both seasons. A slightly wrong charge drags down summer efficiency and winter capacity alike. Without a calculated balance point, backup heat strips can quietly carry the load at several times the cost, and the promised savings never appear. Default thermostat settings may run backup heat unnecessarily or fight the defrost cycle. And in a Troy winter, an outdoor unit resting directly on the ground can end up frozen into its own defrost runoff with the coil locked in ice.

Our experienced technicians design and install against all of it: loads calculated for both seasons, equipment chosen for the actual capacity curve at your winter design temperature, backup heat staged and locked out correctly, the unit elevated for defrost drainage, and the charge weighed and verified rather than guessed. Maintenance Tips for Heat Pump Installation

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Gently hose off the outdoor coil every spring before cooling season
  • Keep an eye on winter bills; a sharp increase usually means the backup heat is running too much
  • Change filters on schedule; low airflow hurts a heat pump in both seasons
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of snow drifts, leaves, and shrubs year-round
  • Expect light frost in winter and a periodic defrost hiss; solid ice means call us
  • Keep temperature settings steady so the backup strips are not needed to catch up
  • Plan two tune-ups a year so the system is verified in both heating and cooling
