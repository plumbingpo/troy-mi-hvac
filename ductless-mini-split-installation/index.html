A ductless mini split solves the problems central systems cannot touch: the garage workshop that bakes all summer, the addition the ductwork never reached, the bonus room that is never quite comfortable, the older Troy, MI house with no ducts at all. Each indoor head connects to its outdoor unit through a slim line set that carries refrigerant, power, and condensate, and with no ducts there are no duct losses. The inverter-driven compressor ramps smoothly up and down instead of slamming on and off, holding temperature within a fraction of a degree while sipping electricity. In heating mode, a modern mini split is a genuine cold-climate heat pump, not just an air conditioner with ambitions.

Good ductless comfort starts at the design stage, not a catalog. We size each zone to what that room actually needs, since an oversized head short-cycles, leaves the air clammy, and never quite holds temperature. When one outdoor unit serves several heads, the engineering gets stricter: capacity has to be split sensibly, and line set length and elevation change must stay inside the manufacturer's tables or the system quietly loses performance. Style matters too, because a wall head, a ceiling cassette, and a low floor unit each affect both airflow and the look of the room. Our experienced technicians walk through the space with you, agree on placements that work physically and visually, and plan line set routes that stay hidden where possible and neat where they show.

On installation day, the details that customers never see are the ones we refuse to skip. Every flare is formed carefully, torqued to spec, and leak-checked, because a leaking flare joint is the number one way mini splits fail. The line set gets a nitrogen pressure test, then a deep vacuum confirmed on a micron gauge, before refrigerant ever moves. We make sure condensate drains with proper fall or a dependable pump, trim the charge for the actual run length, and test each zone in heating and cooling. Why Hire a Professional for Ductless Mini Split Installation?

Mini splits look simple hanging on the wall, and that illusion produces a steady stream of failed installs. The flare connections that join the line set are the system's weak point: under-torqued or badly formed flares weep refrigerant for months, and the first symptom is a head blowing lukewarm air a season later. Skipping the nitrogen pressure test and micron-verified vacuum leaves moisture in a circuit that inverter compressors tolerate poorly. Line sets run past the manufacturer's length and lift tables quietly cost capacity, and a condensate drain without proper fall stains a wall the first humid week.

What separates good ductless work from bad is discipline: sizing. Even the smallest indoor heads are oversized for plenty of bedrooms, and an oversized head cycles constantly, leaves moisture in the air, and never finds a steady state. Troy HVAC sizes zones honestly and installs them with the same care, torquing and testing every flare, proving the line set with nitrogen, pulling a measured vacuum, adjusting charge for the run, and testing each head in both modes. The refrigerant side is federally regulated and the electrical side calls for a properly sized circuit and disconnect, so neither is a weekend project. What you get is a system that stays silent and efficient for fifteen years instead of one that mysteriously underperforms.

How to Prevent Ductless Mini Split Installation Problems

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Brief defrost cycles and a little steam in winter are normal, but a unit encased in ice needs service
  • Clean the washable filter screens in each head about once a month, since they clog faster than furnace filters
  • Do not wait for a musty smell; get the blower wheel and coil cleaned by a pro on a regular basis
  • Get a yearly professional checkup of charge, flare joints, and drainage while any fix is still small
  • Resist turning the system off and on; an inverter is most efficient holding one steady temperature
  • Check the condensate outlet during cooling season to make sure water is actually coming out
  • Give the outdoor unit open space in every season, free of drifts, debris, and mowing clippings
