Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Kalamazoo

Most mid-season breakdowns start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Swap your filter every one to three months, keep plants and shrubs at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and hose off the condenser coil once a year in spring. Then book a professional tune-up each spring and fall, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, central air usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rising energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new system during the worst heat wave of the year.

Small problems don't stay minor when they're ignored. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and just like that a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a few miserable nights.

Typical Heating & Cooling Issues in Kalamazoo

HVAC systems in this area face a familiar set of enemies. Clogged filters quietly choke off airflow, freezing evaporator coils in summer and overheating furnace heat exchangers in winter, damage that builds slowly and out of sight.

Our technicians also run into the full range of component failures in local homes: capacitors that leave the outdoor unit humming but not starting, contactors with pitted points, cracked igniters and dirty flame sensors that lock furnaces out on cold mornings, slow refrigerant leaks that leave the house lukewarm by afternoon, and blower motors worn down by years of restricted airflow.
